## Current research — MSc in Computer Science (UFRGS)

My MSc research at the Federal University of Rio Grande do Sul (UFRGS) investigates the relationship between **control-problem structure** and the computational costs of **Model Predictive Control (MPC)** and **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**.

- **MPC:** study online optimization cost using quadratic-program structure, including Hessian dimensions, KKT-system dimensions, constraints, and solver effort.
- **RL:** study offline training cost, environment transitions, updates, and evaluation at comparable control quality.
- **Dynamics:** investigate whether properties of the system matrix (including spectral radius and norm) help explain the observed costs.
- **Research question:** do structural and dynamical descriptors explain computational difficulty better than state dimension alone?

This is an ongoing empirical research program, **not a claim of general equivalence or superiority between MPC and RL**. **Completing the MSc dissertation and validating its existing evidence are my immediate priorities.**

## Planned research — PhD application in Applied Mathematics (UFRGS, 2027/1)

My selected **doctoral proposal** (not an admission or an enrolled PhD project) is *Observational ambiguity and predictability limits in chaotic systems: mathematical analysis and evaluation of learned predictors*.

- **Research line:** Applied Dynamical Systems (*Sistemas Dinâmicos Aplicados*).
- **Mathematical focus:** observational ambiguity, partial observations, nonlinear dynamics, limits of reconstruction and prediction, and the influence of measurement noise.
- **Computational component:** scientific numerical analysis and critical evaluation of learning-based predictors under clearly defined information constraints.
- **Current status:** proposal selected by me for an intended 2027/1 application; enrollment, supervision and research claims have **not** been confirmed.

**Scientific connection, without conflating the projects:** both research directions study how the mathematical structure of dynamical problems conditions what algorithms can reliably compute, learn, or predict. The MSc focuses on **computational cost in MPC/RL**; the proposed PhD focuses on **mathematical limits of observation and predictability**. They have distinct research questions and deliverables.
